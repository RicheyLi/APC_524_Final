It does not change any result.
  - WP 1.10: dormant sources. A burning cell whose only remaining links into unburned fuel have a rate of zero now (extinction moisture overnight, a full-strength retardant drop) stays BURNING as a dormant source with zero intensity, keeps its link progress and spreads again when the rate returns; it burns out after its fuel's holdover time (`configs/holdover.yaml`, placeholder 12 h, `sim.HoldoverTable`, `sim.load_holdover_times`, recorded in provenance as `holdover_s`). Links blocked by barrier geometry do not count. Both engines. A 10-minute stall on `flat_no_wind` now delays the fire by 10 minutes; before, it ended the fire. Runs without a zero-rate period are unchanged (golden arrays untouched).
  - WP 1.11: edge detection. Every run records `provenance["reached_edge_s"]`, the earliest arrival at a cell within `stencil.max_offset` cells of the grid edge (the cells with a link leaving the grid), or None, with an `"edge"` event (`sim.first_edge_arrival`, `sim.edge_band_mask`). `EnsembleResult.reached_edge_s` holds it per run; `summary.json` carries `reached_edge_s` (run) and `n_reached_edge` / `first_reached_edge_s` (ensemble), `runs.csv` and `ensemble.npz` a `reached_edge_s` column, and `wildfire-sim run` / `ensemble` print a warning that such results are lower bounds. Arrival times are unchanged. With the current defaults `flat_no_wind` reaches the edge band at 7,403 s, not within the 7,200 s the plan assumed, so the acceptance test runs for 7,500 s.
  - WP 1.12: front-normal fireline intensity. `SpreadOutput` gains the optional `heat_per_area` (kJ/m^2, default None; signed-off change to a frozen module). When a cell ignites, the engines estimate the front-normal rate `1 / |grad T|` from a least-squares plane through the arrival times of the cell and its already-burned stencil neighbours (`sim.front_normal_rate`), falling back to the igniting link's rate when the plane is undetermined, and set the intensity to `heat_per_area * R_n` (or the head intensity times `R_n / max_k ros`), capped at the head value. `grid.intensity` shows it while a cell flames and the new `RunResult.front_intensity` keeps it for every burned cell. `sim.flame_length_from_intensity` gives Byram's (1959) flame length with constants in `configs/flame_length.yaml`. Arrival times are unchanged. Measured against the analytic ellipse (`docs/lattice_study.md` section 6): within 3 % on a round fire and 20 % at a length-to-breadth ratio of 1.5; at a ratio of 3 the flank is 27-45 % too hot and a head between stencil directions reads 0.4 of the head value, the known lattice limit left to WP 2.1d (strict expected failure).
  - WP 1.13: urban cells burn. `LandUse.URBAN.burnable` is now True and `Grid.burnable_mask` follows it (signed-off change to two frozen modules); an urban cell carries a surrogate fuel model and, after the spread rule has run, the engines multiply its rate of spread by `ros_factor` and its residence time by `residence_factor`, so every rule is scaled alike. New module `wildfire_sim.urban` (`UrbanModel`, `load_urban_model`) and `configs/urban.yaml` (placeholders, team assumptions: TL3, 0.5, 30), recorded in provenance as `urban`. `with_town` writes the surrogate fuel model into its town cells (new argument `urban_fuel_model`). With `ros_factor` 0 urban cells are unburnable, as before. Water and roads are unchanged. **Results change for any grid with urban cells:** fire now runs through a town, where before only its rim was exposed. Grids without urban cells are bit-identical (golden arrays untouched; none of them has a town).
- Phase 2 (Rothermel and real data, see `docs/plan.md` section 12):
  - WP 2.1a: native Rothermel (1972) surface fire model with the Albini (1976) revisions, vectorized in NumPy (`wildfire_sim.spread.rothermel`): reaction intensity, propagating flux, heat sink, wind and slope factors, moisture damping, live fuel moisture of extinction, size-class weighted net load, and the dynamic herbaceous transfer of Scott & Burgan (2005). `surface_fire` evaluates the fuel bed once per fuel model present and returns head rate of spread (m/s), Byram intensity (kW/m), flame length (m), heat per unit area (kJ/m^2) and residence time `384 / sigma` min (Anderson 1969); `head_fire` takes a combined wind-and-slope factor for WP 2.1b; Rothermel's wind limit `0.9 I_R` is optional (`wind_limit_on`, off by default). `configs/fuel_models.csv` holds the 40 Scott & Burgan and 13 Anderson fuel models with sources. Agrees with the BehavePlus engine (`behave` via `pyrothermel`) to 0.01 % in rate and intensity for all 53 models over six moisture/wind/slope cases (`tests/test_spread_rothermel.py`; the cross-check skips where `pyrothermel` is not installed).
  - WP 2.1b: the `"rothermel"` spread rule (`wildfire_sim.spread.rothermel.RothermelRule`). The 10 m wind is divided by 1.15 to the 20-ft wind (Turner and Lawson 1978) and reduced to midflame height by the unsheltered or sheltered wind adjustment factor (Albini and Baughman 1979; Andrews 2012); wind and slope factors are added as vectors, giving the direction of maximum spread; the rate toward each stencil direction follows the fire ellipse with Anderson's (1983) length-to-breadth ratio, capped at 8; rates, head intensity and flame length are multiplied by `1 - retardant`, and `heat_per_area` is returned for front-normal intensity. New module `wildfire_sim.spread.ellipse` and parameter file `configs/rothermel.yaml` (canopy height 15 m and crown ratio 0.4 are placeholders; Rothermel's wind limit off, an open decision). Agrees with the BehavePlus engine to better than 1e-7 in head rate, direction and directional rates; on flat uniform fuel it gives exactly the elliptical rule of the physics tests, and simulated it burns round without wind and within 10 % of the analytic ellipse at a ratio of 1.5 (`tests/test_spread_ellipse.py`, `tests/test_physics.py`).
  - WP 2.1c: dead fuel moisture from temperature and humidity (`wildfire_sim.spread.moisture`). Equilibrium moisture content by Simard's (1968) regression as printed in Cohen & Deeming (1985); fuel-level air corrected for sunshine by the NFDRS state-of-weather code; 1-h and 10-h moisture as 1.03 and 1.28 times the equilibrium moisture (NFDRS without fuel sticks), so the 10-h value is 1.243 times the 1-h value. `with_dead_fuel_moisture` updates a `Weather`, `apply_hourly` a `WeatherSeries` (one breakpoint per hour, optional cloudiness code per hour). Parameters with sources in `configs/fuel_moisture.yaml`. Reproduces the Wood Handbook's equilibrium moisture table (Table 4-2) within 1.0 percentage point from 30 to 110 deg F; on a synthetic diurnal series 1-h moisture peaks at 03:00 and bottoms at 15:00 (`tests/test_moisture.py`).
  - WP 2.1d: elongated fires on the lattice. Lattice-fitted directional rates (`wildfire_sim.spread.ellipse.fit_rates`): for a length-to-breadth ratio and head bearing, the rates whose lattice polygon minimizes the largest radial error against the fire ellipse, about half that of rates sampled from the ellipse. Precomputed per stencil on ratios 1-2.6 and canonical head bearings 0-45 deg (`configs/lattice_fit_8.csv`, `configs/lattice_fit_16.csv`, written by `scripts/lattice_study.py --fit-tables`), mapped to any bearing by the square's symmetries and interpolated (`LatticeFitTable`); a round fire keeps its rates exactly. The `"rothermel"` rule uses them (`lattice_rates: fitted` in `configs/rothermel.yaml`, `"sampled"` for the old rates) and caps the ratio at what the lattice represents within 10 %: 2.1 on 16 neighbours (1.5 with sampled rates) and 1.4 on 8 (1.2), keeping the head rate. Runs record `provenance["lb_capped_cell_hours"]` (also in `summary.json`) and `wildfire-sim run` warns when the cap applied. `scripts/lattice_study.py --fitted` and `docs/lattice_study.md` section 7 hold the study: head and flank within 10 % of the analytic ellipse at head bearings 0, 13.3, 22.5 and 45 deg up to the cap; front-normal intensity re-measured with fitted rates (`tests/test_intensity.py`), with the head between stencil directions at the cap still at 0.74-0.84 of the head intensity (strict expected failure). Constant and Alexandridis runs are unchanged (golden arrays untouched). Fitted rates reach 2.1, not 3, which reopens the choice of front-propagation method (`docs/plan.md` section 13).
  - WP 2.2: LANDFIRE fuel and canopy layers (`wildfire_sim.data.landfire`). `fetch_landfire` gets FBFM40, CC, CH, CBH and CBD for a WGS84 bounding box and a named version from the LANDFIRE Product Service job API (submit, poll, download; the calls its web page makes, checked 2026-11-14 and by a live test), and `ingest_archived` clips GeoTIFFs of an archived version that a person downloads. Both cache GeoTIFFs and a manifest (version, box, access time, file and band per product, SHA-256) under `data/cache/<key>/`; a second request reads the cache. `check_version` refuses a version whose disturbance years reach the fire's year (`configs/landfire_versions.yaml`, from the LANDFIRE version comparison table): LF2016 is refused for fire A (August 2016) and LF2014, archived, is the version to use. `grid_codes` maps FBFM40 to fuel model and land use (urban 91 with the urban surrogate fuel, water 98, no fuel for 92, 93, 99). The LFPS email address comes from `WILDFIRE_SIM_LANDFIRE_EMAIL` and is never cached. Tests are offline (a fake HTTP client; the archive test needs `rasterio`), plus one live test marked `network`.
  - `scripts/demo_real_fire.py`: runs a built region's fire on its real grid, ignited at the recorded point and time, and compares it with the observations (burned area, Jaccard and Sorensen overlap, VIIRS arrival-interval errors) with a map in `runs/`; uncalibrated (Phase 3). Documented in `docs/data.md`.
  - WP 2.9: break crossing (`wildfire_sim.breaks`, `configs/breach.yaml`; `sim.py`, `sim_fast.py`). Wilson's (1988) breach probability with a 0.18 MW/m floor; break cells and constructed lines with a width hold as barriers until breached, decided once per cell against a uniform number from a fourth child of the run's `SeedSequence` (the first three unchanged) by the hottest adjacent ignited cell; a breached cell ignites when the fire would have crossed it. `"breach"` events and `provenance["breaks"]`. Both engines; the Numba engine exposes its cell-major link arrays as `progress_kn` and `thresholds_kn`. Grids without breaks are bit-identical.
  - WP 2.8: validation and strategy metrics (`wildfire_sim.metrics`): `burned_area_ha`, `consensus_mask`, `jaccard`, `sorensen`, `area_ratio`, `arrival_interval_error` (the interval score of section 7 over a detection's footprint, never-reached detections scored against the window end), `asset_loss`, `suppression_cost` with `ResourceUse` records, and seeded percentile bootstrap intervals `bootstrap_ci` and `paired_difference_ci`, whose coverage is checked in a simulation test.
  - WP 2.7: observations of the validation fires (`wildfire_sim.data.validation`). `fetch_ignition` (IRWIN through WFIGS), `fetch_daily_perimeters` (NIFC Operational Data Archive, empty before 2022) and `fetch_viirs` (VIIRS 375 m fire masks and geolocation of Suomi NPP, NOAA-20 and NOAA-21 through CMR and the NASA Earthdata cloud, the geolocation read by HTTP byte ranges; a NASA Earthdata token from `EARTHDATA_TOKEN` or `data/.earthdata_token`), all cached. `FireObservations.observed_burn_mask(grid, until=None)` (MTBS, or every approved daily perimeter posted by `until`) and `detections(grid)` (first detections with `(t_clear, t_detect, row, col)`; `t_clear` the last earlier overpass that saw the cell clear, so cloud and glint never count; `first_detections` holds the rule). `scripts/download_data.py --observations`; `Region.irwin_id`; `h5py` added to the `geo` extra; the weekly network job also takes `EARTHDATA_TOKEN`. The Lake Fire: 654 first detections, 98 % within 1 km of its MTBS perimeter.
  - WP 2.6: cached study regions (`wildfire_sim.data.cache`, `scripts/download_data.py`, `configs/regions.yaml`, `docs/data.md`, `.github/workflows/network.yml`). One command builds a region's `grid.npz`, `canopy.npz`, `weather.yaml`, perimeter and `manifest.json` (every source's manifest and each file's SHA-256) from its MTBS perimeter plus a 3 km buffer, through WPs 2.2-2.5; `--verify` rebuilds from the caches and compares hashes. Fire A (`lake_2024`) builds to 815 x 978 cells; its 5 km crop and weather are committed under `tests/fixtures/`. The build refuses a grid whose north is more than 1 deg from true north (EPSG:5070 is 14.5 deg off at 120 W; LANDFIRE's service delivers a request-centred Albers, 0.09 deg off). `fetch_mtbs_perimeter` reads MTBS perimeters (reused by WP 2.7). A weekly CI job runs the `network` tests. `docs/data.md` records access method, licence and attribution per source.
  - WP 2.5: roads, water and buildings (`wildfire_sim.data.osm`, `configs/osm.yaml`). `Grid` gains the signed-off optional layer `break_width_m` (m; zeros when absent, older grid files still load). `fetch_osm` gets OpenStreetMap features for a bounding box through `osmnx` (retrying with backoff when Overpass is busy) and caches them as GeoJSON with a SHA-256 manifest; `classify` applies the barrier rule (water polygons, motorways, trunk roads and features at least 15 m wide are unburnable; narrower roads and streams are breaks of their width); `rasterize_features` burns them onto the LANDFIRE grid, lines made 4-connected so no stencil move crosses them at a corner; `apply_to_grid` writes land use, `break_width_m` and `asset_value = buildings x usd_per_building`. README states the building value and the OSM attribution. Tests are offline except one live Overpass download marked `network`; the rasterizing tests need the `geo` extra.
  - HRRR hourly weather (WP 2.4 follow-up; `wildfire_sim.data.hrrr`, `data/weather.py`, `data/cache.py`, `configs/regions.yaml`). `fetch_hrrr_point` reads each hour's HRRR analysis from NOAA's AWS archive, five fields by HTTP byte range from the `.idx` offsets (2 m temperature and humidity, 10 m wind, total cloud cover), decodes them with GDAL's GRIB driver through `rasterio` (no new dependency), samples them bilinearly at a point and caches them with a SHA-256 manifest. - Phase 3 (calibration and validation, see `docs/plan.md` section 12):
  - WP 3.1: calibration (`wildfire_sim.calibration`, `scripts/calibrate.py`, `configs/calibration.yaml`, `configs/calibrated.yaml`, `docs/calibration.md`). `RothermelRule` takes two calibration factors, also through `spread.params`: `ros_multiplier` (head rate, intensity and flame length times the factor, FARSITE's adjustment factor) and `lb_scale` (`LB' = 1 + lb_scale (LB - 1)` before the caps; `ellipse.length_to_breadth` gains the argument); both default to 1, which leaves every result bit-identical. The objective is `w_o (1 - J) + w_a E` at the end of the early-growth window, `E` the mean VIIRS arrival-interval error over the window length, averaged over fixed seeds; a trial in which a run reaches the grid edge is rejected, not scored; the multivariate TPE study is seeded and the objective is evaluated on a grid around the optimum. Targets: a built region's daily perimeters and detections (`real_fire`, `observed_target`, now also used by `scripts/demo_real_fire.py`) or a synthetic observed fire (`synthetic_target`, `synthetic_detections`). A synthetic fire's parameters are recovered within 4 % at head bearings 0, 13.3, 22.5 and 45 degrees, also against the analytic ellipse; `lb_scale` is not identified above the lattice cap. `configs/calibrated.yaml` is a PLACEHOLDER (neutral values): fire A's region was not available, so no study has been run. `scripts/bench.py --region / --grid` times a real fire with the Rothermel rule; crop measurements and the study's sizing in `docs/performance.md`. The CI Numba job also runs the calibration tests with optuna.
  - WP 3.2: Morris screening (`wildfire_sim.sensitivity`, `scripts/sensitivity.py`, `configs/sensitivity.yaml`, `docs/sensitivity.md`). The elementary-effects method of Morris (1991) with `mu*` (Campolongo et al. 2007), implemented in NumPy: trajectories on a `p`-level grid (`morris_design`), elementary effects, `mu`, `mu*`, `sigma` and the rank per input (`morris_indices`), discrete inputs (stencil, kernel shape) mapped to equal bins so every step changes them. The study screens burned area at the end of a window and the head arrival time (first ignition at a given distance from the ignition, defined in any wind direction and never affected by the grid edge; a run that never gets there is left out and counted, not filled in) over wind speed and direction, 1-h, 10-h/100-h, live herbaceous and live woody moisture, the rate multiplier (`ensemble.ScaledRateRule`), the kernel shape, the stencil, a deliberately inert control (air temperature, which the engine never reads) and the section-13 placeholders reachable without engine changes (wind limit, canopy height, crown ratio, break widths, holdover, urban factors, breach floor, tree threshold; parameter files through a temporary `WILDFIRE_SIM_CONFIGS` copy). Common random numbers within a screening; ranges and their sources in `configs/sensitivity.yaml`. On a synthetic grass-shrub grid and the committed 5 km crop of fire A the inert control has `mu*` exactly 0 and ranks last on both outputs and both seeds; the rate multiplier and wind speed rank first and second everywhere, the stencil third for burned area. Edge-reaching runs are counted per run and per elementary effect. The study regenerates in about 3 minutes with 8 workers.
  - Fix: `ensemble.ScaledRateRule` forwards the inner rule's `lb_capped` mask. It hid it, so a run with a rate multiplier, including every perturbed ensemble member, recorded 0 lattice-capped cell-hours (`lb_capped_cell_hours`) whatever the wind. Arrival times are unchanged.
  - Fix: `data.validation.fetch_viirs` keeps the catalogue's list of overpasses for a box and period in the cache manifest (`listings`), so a region whose observations are cached loads without any network access (`fetch_observations`, `scripts/calibrate.py`, `scripts/demo_real_fire.py` on a compute node). Before, every call queried NASA's catalogue even when every overpass was cached. A cache built before this change asks the catalogue once more and then stores the answer.

## Changed

- **Faster runs on real grids, results unchanged** (WP 2.9 follow-up): a breach now updates the engine locally (`_FireEngine.update_barriers`: blocked links, link rates and stall flags near the breached cells) instead of re-running the spread rule over the whole grid; the engine is first refreshed in the ignition step, so the hours of weather before the fire cost nothing; and only held break cells next to newly ignited cells are checked. A 24-hour run of fire A on its 815 x 978 grid went from over 50 minutes to about 60 s, a 5 km crop's 4 hours from 5.7 to 0.6 s; runs are bit-identical (tested on both engines). Numbers in `docs/performance.md`.
- **Fire B is the 2020 Silverado Fire** (Orange County, CA), chosen after vetting four Santa Ana candidates on their MTBS records, disturbance after their pre-fire LANDFIRE version, early VIIRS overpasses and HRRR hours (`docs/plan.md` section 13). Region `silverado_2020` added to `configs/regions.yaml`, its 5 km crop and weather to `tests/fixtures/`. HRRR covers both fires' weather windows, so the plan now calls for an HRRR reader (new open item).
- **Fire A is now the 2024 Lake Fire** (Santa Barbara County, CA), replacing the 2016 Chimney Fire, whose pre-fire LANDFIRE fuels (LF2014) could not be obtained: the map viewer offers LF2016 and later, the Product Service serves only LF2014's disturbance layers, and ScienceBase has no LF2014 fuel model. LF2016 cannot stand in, since 98 % of the Chimney Fire's perimeter is already fire-disturbed in it. ## Fixed

- A region's `weather.yaml` and the committed weather fixture were written with the platform's line ends, so their SHA-256 differed between Windows and Linux; `data.cache` now writes them with `\n` (`write_yaml_lf`), found while building fire A's observations (WP 2.7).

- `data.terrain.terrain_for_grid` returned aspects just below 360 deg that became 360.0 when stored as float32, so `Grid.validate` refused the grid (found building the Lake Fire region, WP 2.6); the aspect is now cast with `aspect_float32`, which wraps 360 to 0.

- CI type check failed on `main` after WP 2.2: `ty` runs without the optional `geo` extra and could not resolve the `rasterio` imports of `wildfire_sim.data.landfire`. The geo modules are now imported by name (`landfire.optional_import`, as `sim_fast` imports numba), and the tests get them with `pytest.importorskip`.
- CodeQL workflow: `init` and `analyze` are on the same `github/codeql-action` version again (a Dependabot bump had moved only `analyze`, which makes the job fail); Dependabot now groups the two.
- `ensemble.mean_ci` returned NaN bounds for finite samples near the float64 limit (found by a property test).
- `link_rate_of_spread`: a knight link (16-neighbour stencil) now uses the harmonic mean over the four cells its segment crosses. Using only the two end cells let fire tunnel through one cell of slow fuel at each edge of a strip (delay 12 % short).
- `Grid.apply_unburnable` no longer overwrites BURNING or BURNED cells (a fireline placed under a burning cell erased it from the burned area).
- The memoryless ignition kernel (`p = 1 - exp(-R dt / d)`) moved the fire front 3 to 4.5 times faster than `R` and depended on the time step. It remains available as `arrival_shape = 1` only.
- `ScenarioConfig.n_steps` lost a step to floating-point rounding (3600 s at 0.1 s gave 35999).

- `pyproject.toml` authors/maintainers arrays were missing commas.
